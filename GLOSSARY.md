# Glossary

Vocabulary for All Contribute. No implementation details here.

## Content

- **Block definition** — a registered block with its properties and behaviour rules. The unit of player-created content alongside recipes.
- **Built-in block** — a block owned by the admin account under the `builtin:` namespace. Immutable for everyone.
- **Player block** — a block under the `player:<username>:<id>` namespace. Only its creator can change it.
- **Behaviour** — the category a block is filed under: `solid`, `transparent` (light passes through it), `glowing` (it gives off light), `decorative` or `useful`. A **useful block** is one meant to be *used*: it answers when a player interacts with it (the bed sets your respawn, the beacon switches its light column) rather than only occupying a cell. The category is a label; the answer itself is the block's script.
- **Behaviour rules** — what a block does, expressed declaratively for now (support rule, incompatibility list) plus a behaviour category: `solid`, `transparent` (light passes through it), `glowing`, `decorative`, or `useful` — a block you *use* rather than just walk into, whose answer lives in its script (the bed sets your respawn, the beacon switches its light column). Player-authored executable behaviour is a later phase (ADR 0001).
- **Support rule** — the condition on the block beneath a placement position that governs whether it may be placed: any, solid-only, or none (requires empty below).
- **Incompatible blocks** — a block can name other blocks it must never touch. Placing next to one is refused and the item stays with the player.
- **Placement outcome** — the three results of a placement attempt: placed, refused (block returned to the player), or transformed by the block beneath (player-controlled via behaviour).
- **Conflict** — two active block behaviours demanding contradictory world changes (e.g. one shoves neighbours right, another shoves them left). The game must never crash; the blocks involved break instead. Reaches full meaning with executable behaviour (ADR 0001).
- **Recipe** — ingredients consume item stacks and create a result item; authored in-game like blocks.
- **Item** — what a player holds, carries, and consumes: the unit of the held-thing registry, distinct from a block (what occupies a voxel). Every block has an item that places it; tools, weapons, food, and materials are items with no voxel form.
- **Item kind** — what an item is for: placeable (`block`), mining aid (`tool`), attack (`weapon`), eaten (`food`), or crafting input only (`material`). Stats belong to the kind: speed, damage, healing, durability.
- **Programme** — the one declarative act a placeable item performs: the block it puts into the world. Authored by the item's creator as data.
- **Block script** — what a block does when a player uses it, authored by the block's creator and run by the server. Behaviour rules still decide where a block may stand; a script decides what happens when it is used.
- **Interaction** — using the block under the crosshair (**E**). A block with no script does nothing when used; a block whose script fails to run says so, with the interpreter's complaint, so a broken block is never just a dead one.
- **Menu** — a block script's question to the player: a title and a list of choices. Making a choice is a second interaction with the same block.
- **Break hook** — the half of a block script that runs as the block is destroyed, while its memory and store are still there to read. It can speak, move the player who swung, and hand its store over to them; then the voxel, its memory, its store and its light column are deleted together. A hook that fails never stops the break.
- **Look** — the one player choice about visual detail: *flat* (everything painted from its registered colour) or *detailed* (the appearance its publisher supplied). One choice, so a block and an item can never disagree about how they look.
- **Texture, model, icon** — the appearance attached to published content: a block's surface image, the shape it asks for, an item's picture. A block's texture is its **four sides**; it may also name a separate picture for its **upper face** and **underside**, falling back to the side image when it does not. All optional; the registered colour is the fallback for anyone on *flat*. Built-in content ships its own pictures, so *detailed* is the view the game is actually played in.
- **Built-in item** — an item owned by SYSTEM under the `builtin:` namespace. Immutable for everyone.
- **Player item** — an item under the `player:<username>:<ns>:<id>` namespace. Only its creator can change it.
- **Appearance** — the four colours an account owns for its avatar: head, body, legs and eyes. Stored per user, relayed on presence frames, and re-coloured live. Eyes are drawn on the head, so they are the only part that says which way a body is facing. The look is account data, so it arrives with the account: a world opened before you signed in repaints your body the moment you do.
- **Entity / mob** — a creature the world object simulates: it walks on the surface, and is either hostile (seeks the nearest living player within a 56-block sphere, damages them when the 3D gap closes to 1.8 blocks) or peaceful (wanders). Nobody spawns one: each tick adds one to the **spawn ring**, 40-50 blocks from a live player, while fewer than 12 exist, and any mob more than 96 blocks from every connected player is forgotten — otherwise a far-wandered mob holds a slot forever and the world stops producing new ones. Range is measured in all three axes, so altitude is protection; only the mob's *steering* is horizontal. Hostility is a property of the creature, not of the player.
- **Health** — a player's 0–20 life total, owned by the server. Zero means dead: no movement, no damage, and the only way back is respawn.
- **Respawn** — returning a dead player to their spawn anchor with full health: the surface spawn column, unless a block script has set an anchor for that account (the bed does).
- **Spawn anchor** — the one position an account wakes up at after dying. Walking never sets it; a block script does, and it beats the account's spawn column. It survives the bed being broken, so the next bed you sleep in simply moves it.
- **First person / third person** — two cameras, one body. First person looks out of the eye; third person moves a camera behind the shoulders and draws the player's own body, built from the same parts as every other avatar and wearing the same things: the account's saved colours, and the item its owner has selected — a cube painted with that item's own icon when the registry carries one, with its colour otherwise. So the body you turn around and look at is the body every other player sees. Only the camera changes: the body, its physics, what the crosshair picks and what the presence frames report all stay on the eye camera.
- **Presence identity** — who a world socket belongs to: the signed-in account, or a one-off guest token when nobody signed in. The socket is bound to it when it connects, from the session, and a frame's own name is never trusted; signing in or out re-opens the socket so that binding happens again. Two players who share an identity share one avatar.
- **Spawn column** — the ground a new account first stands on, picked from the account name so two players who join at the same moment do not start inside each other. Any position the player has actually walked to wins over it.

## World

- **World** — one shared voxel world: columns of blocks addressed by `(x, y, z)` with y measured upward from an unbreakable bedrock floor. Base terrain (land, caves, trees) is a deterministic function of coordinates; what differs from it is player-made.
- **Voxel** — one unit cube of the world at a coordinate triple. The word "cell" is no longer used: the world is 3D end to end.
- **Edit** — a player-caused world change: a placement or a cleared voxel, recorded at its `(x, y, z)`. The world is base terrain plus edits.
- **Voxel record (composite id)** — the number a voxel cell actually stores for a block, and the entry in a thread's voxel tables that turns a block name into it. The numbers are positional and shared: every thread that touches voxels has to use the same one for the same name, or one thread's placement reads as another's different block. A name only encodes when its record slot decodes back to that name; blocks the terrain never generates have to be aligned to the numbers the server-side engine already used, never renumbered, because an unrecorded name encodes as air and a placement writes nothing and stays silent.
- **Voxel state** — what a block script remembers about a particular voxel. It belongs to the place, not to the player or the block type: two identical blocks in two places are independent, and breaking the block forgets it.
- **Voxel store** — the item stacks a block script keeps inside the voxel it stands in. Like the script's memory it belongs to the place, so two identical blocks can hold different things; when the voxel is broken its contents go to whoever broke it and the store is deleted with it.
- **Voxel light** — the brightness stored in every cell of the world, in two channels: daylight that enters from the open sky and fades two levels per block, and block light that spreads one level per block from anything with a light value. Light only travels through cells that are not full cubes, so a wall blocks it and glass does not. The mesher bakes it into each face; this is what a light-emitting block actually does, and it replaced the emissive glow cubes that only made a block look bright.
- **Light column** — a column of coloured light running from a voxel to the top of the world, switched on and off by the block standing there. It is part of the world's state, so it survives a reload and is there for a player who arrives later.
- **Derived world row** — a row kept *about* a voxel to make reads cheap or a late joiner current (catch-up log, block script memory, light column, voxel store), as opposed to the row that says what stands there. It only means anything while the edit table agrees with it; once the voxel it describes is gone, the row is stale and must not be allowed to paint anything.
- **World resync** — the admin's repair switch: delete the derived rows that lost their voxel, replay every surviving edit and light column to everyone, and have each client rebuild its world from what the database says. Nothing real is touched; a block a browser painted that the database never had disappears.
- **Day cycle, clock** — the 24-minute in-game day, as a phase from 0 (midnight) to 1. Nobody stores it per frame: un-dialled, each client derives the same phase from its own wall clock, which is why a shared sky costs no traffic. The admin's dial is one row (`world_clock`: the phase, the real-world moment it was dialled at, whether the cycle runs); clients derive *phase plus elapsed*, or hold the phase while stopped. The dial is reached with **+**, which opens the **admin settings** dialog for the admin account alone — the pause menu carries no admin control, so every player's pause menu is the same menu. A socket is handed the clock the moment it connects and every client also *asks* for it at join, on reconnect and when that dialog opens: a push can be lost, and one lost frame used to leave a single player in a different day from everyone else. Only the admin may write it.

## Ownership and hosting

- **Inventory** — a logged-in player's server-owned item stacks. Placement debits, refusal never debits, breaking credits.
- **Bag order** — a player's own arrangement of their stacks. The hotbar is the front of the bag, so arranging the bag arranges the hotbar. Only the player arranges their own.
- **Landing** — the main menu you open the site on: it carries **Log in** / **Create account**, the Server picker, Settings and Instructions, and it stays open until **Join**, which needs a session. Signing in from the landing opens the auth form *over* it, so there is always a Join button to press afterwards.
- **World (server)** — one scoped voxel world: blocks, clock, mobs, light columns, voxel state and saved player positions all belong to it. `global` is the shared world every player joins by default; any signed-in player may create up to three of their own from the main menu and delete them again. An unscoped request means `global` and behaves exactly as it always did.
- **World visibility** — who may reach a world: `private` (only the device that made it, recognised by a hashed device key), `lan` (that device plus any requester whose IP shares its local /24), `public` (anyone who can reach the site's API). The rule is enforced by the Worker, not the menu.
- **Device key** — a random 32-hex string a browser generates once and keeps in its own storage, sent as `X-AC-Device` on every request. A `private` world stores only its SHA-256 hash, so a leaked row cannot be replayed into access.
- **Admin** — the single account allowed to change item counts on accounts (grant, remove, clear). Arranging one's own inventory is not an admin act. The admin may also dial any world's clock and resync any world; a world's owner may do both for their own.
